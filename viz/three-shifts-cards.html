Shift 1

Plan Around Water Realities

Align agricultural decisions with local water conditions.

Some countries can sustainably expand irrigation and production. Others will need to improve water productivity, shift toward less water-intensive crops, or rely more strategically on trade. The most effective agricultural systems are designed around long-term water realities - not short-term production targets alone.

Illustration

Shift 2

Treat Irrigation as a Service

Long-term performance depends on reliable delivery, not infrastructure alone

Many irrigation systems struggle because investments focus on construction, while maintenance, accountability, and water delivery remain weak. Systems perform better when irrigation is treated as an ongoing service - with clear responsibilities, reliable financing, and measurable results.

Illustration

Shift 3

Use Better Data to Guide Decisions

Use data to make trade-offs visible - and manageable.

Satellite imagery, remote sensing, and digital monitoring tools are helping countries understand where irrigation expansion makes sense, where water stress is rising, and where investments can deliver the greatest returns. Better data makes trade-offs more visible - and decisions more strategic.
