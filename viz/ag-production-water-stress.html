What to notice

  • Top producers, tight water budgets. India, Pakistan, China, the western U.S., Spain, Iran, Egypt and Turkey all appear deep green and densely hatched - large shares of global food calories grown where freshwater is already over-allocated.
  • Untapped potential where the water is. Most of Sub-Saharan Africa shows low hatching and modest production today - rainfall is sufficient, yields are below potential, and the gap between current and possible output is the largest in the world.
  • A global trade-system risk, not a local one. Because the heaviest-producing regions are also the most water-stressed, a drought or policy shock in one of them ripples through international markets - importers thousands of kilometers away feel it on the shelf.
  • Climate change is tightening the bind. Under business-as-usual scenarios, baseline stress rises further in exactly the breadbaskets that already feed the most people, narrowing the window to shift toward water-smart production.

Sources: FAOSTAT food balance sheets 2019 (production aggregated as billion kilocalories across crops, livestock and processed products); WRI Aqueduct 3.0 (baseline water stress, % of available freshwater withdrawn).
